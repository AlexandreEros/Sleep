\part{The Overfitted-Brain Hypothesis: A Unifying Account}\label{part:obh}

We now have the pieces: sleep is structured (see Part~\ref{part:science}), and dreams reflect that structure (see Chapter~\ref{ch:phenomenology}). Part~\ref{part:obh} synthesizes these into a unified computational account: the \emph{Overfitted-Brain Hypothesis}. Sleep is not just maintenance; it is an \emph{algorithm}. NREM consolidates; REM de-overfits. Under this analogy, dreams are the ``training data'' that make that possible.

\medskip

A word of caution: every generation uses its most advanced technology as the metaphor for mind. Descartes had hydraulics; Freud had steam engines; the mid-20th century had switchboards; the late 20th century had computers; today we have machine learning. Each metaphor sounds quaint in retrospect, but each revealed something real. The ML metaphor foregrounds a genuine problem: \emph{overfitting and the need for generalization}. We find it unlikely that future neuroscience will describe sleep in precisely these ML-inspired terms; however, the computational problem (how a learning system avoids memorizing noise) is real, ancient, and universal. The Overfitted-Brain Hypothesis is one attempt from our generation to name it.
