\part{Dreams as Cultural Evidence: What We Knew Before the Lab}\label{part:culture}

Before neuroscience, humans knew dreams mattered \- so much so that they built entire healing and decision-making systems around them. We argue that this intuition was, though often exaggerated, directionally correct: dreams \emph{do} play a fundamental role. But to understand \emph{why}, we must first understand sleep itself. This part surveys the cultural history of dream interpretation (Chapter~\ref{ch:why_dreams_matter}), the scientific history of oneirology (Chapter~\ref{ch:history_oneirology}), and a myth-busting executive summary of what remains from pre-scientific dream lore (Chapter~\ref{ch:what_survived}).

\chapter{Why Dreams Matter: A Cultural Survey}\label{ch:why_dreams_matter}

Dreams are more than nocturnal cinema: they sit where private phenomenology meets learning and creativity. This section first sorts the canonical ``dream discoveries'' into what is well-sourced versus apocryphal, then reframes the romantic \emph{Eureka} trope in light of what sleep science actually shows about insight and memory reorganization.

The cultural meanings of sleep and dreams are vast and multifaceted: healing and divination; ethics and law; philosophy and identity; spiritual training and bodily technique.
