\section{The Objective Revolution: The Discovery of REM Sleep}\label{sec:rem_revolution}

The single most important event in modern sleep science occurred in 1953. Researchers \citet{aserinsky1953rem} discovered that dreaming ``typically occur[s] during rapid eye movement (REM) sleep''. This discovery was a paradigm shift.

The discovery of REM sleep was made using electroencephalography (EEG) to monitor brain activity during sleep. Aserinsky and Kleitman observed that during certain periods of sleep, the eyes moved rapidly beneath closed eyelids, and these periods were associated with low-amplitude, mixed-frequency brain waves similar to those seen during wakefulness. They also noted that subjects awakened during these REM periods reported vivid dreams, while those awakened during non-REM (NREM) sleep reported little to no dream content \citep{aserinsky1953rem}. This correlation between REM sleep and dreaming provided the first objective evidence linking specific brain activity patterns to the subjective experience of dreaming.

For the first time, the subjective, internal state of dreaming was linked to an \textit{objective, physiological correlate} that could be measured in a laboratory. This finding moved oneirology (the study of dreams) from the analyst's couch to the neuroscience lab, providing a ``foundation'' for all subsequent scientific investigation.
