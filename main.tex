\begin{abstract}
Sleep and dreaming, while biologically universal, have long posed a functional puzzle. This paper synthesizes findings from neuroscience, comparative biology, and computational modeling to posit an integrative model of sleep as a \emph{multi-stage learning algorithm}. We propose that NREM sleep consolidates episodic memory via neural replay, while REM sleep generalizes knowledge and prevents “cognitive overfitting”. Dreams \- the subjective phenomenology of this offline process \- provide the primary window into how brains learn in the long term. We review the phenomenological evidence for this model (e.g., the Tetris effect in hypnagogia), the cross-species scope of REM-/REM-like states, and stage-selective deficits under deprivation. We also consider pathological (psychosis under sleep loss) and practical (lucid dreaming, targeted dream incubation) implications, concluding that sleep is a non-negotiable algorithm for consolidation and generalization in complex, learning nervous systems.
\end{abstract}
