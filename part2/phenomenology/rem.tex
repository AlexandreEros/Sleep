\section{REM Dreams}\label{sec:rem_dreams}

While NREM sleep can contain thought-like ``mentation,'' the classic, immersive dream experience occurs during REM.\@ The phenomenology of REM dreams is famously characterized by three properties \citep{hobson1977activation}:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Bizarreness (Incongruity):} Characters, times, and places are unstable and merge illogically. You may be in your childhood home, but it is also a spaceship, and your 3rd-grade teacher is there, but she is also your mother.
    \item \textbf{Intense Emotion:} Dreams are hyper-emotional, with the limbic system (especially the amygdala) being highly active. The most common emotions are anxiety, fear, and wonder.
    \item \textbf{Uncritical Acceptance:} Despite the profound bizarreness, the dreaming self almost completely accepts the simulation as real. The prefrontal cortex, responsible for logic, planning, and self-awareness, is significantly downregulated \citep{revonsuo2013important}.
\end{enumerate}
